How to create a Yealico rule in one minute with iYegen

No selectors. No regex. No JSON editor. Here is the complete automatic flow, exactly as you will use it in the app.

Step 1 — Paste the site's URL

Open iYegen and paste the address of the site's list page — the page that shows the covers/titles you browse. Tap the magic wand button 🪄.

Behind the scenes, an invisible browser loads the page with JavaScript enabled, waits for it to settle, and captures the real, post-JavaScript page — the same page Yealico would work with.

Step 2 — Confirm the index

iYegen finds the repeating items on the page (the cards), and pulls the title, cover and link from each one. Categories are detected and applied automatically, and pagination is figured out by comparing pages — not by guessing.

Step 3 — Follow the guided chain

Now tap an item to open its detail page. The app captures it and asks one simple question:

What does this page contain?
🎬 Video · 🖼 Gallery · ➡️ Neither — keep going

The app suggests the answer based on what it measured (a video player found? a photo grid?) — you just confirm, or tap through to the next layer and it asks again. Pages are archived in order: 1st = list, 2nd = detail, 3rd = video/gallery page.

Step 4 — Get the JSON

That's it. iYegen assembles the complete site rule: index, search, categories, tags when the site has them, detail, video or gallery, and pagination — a single JSON, built from proven structures, ready to use. The description of the detail even carries the video link, the way experienced rule authors do it.

Step 5 — Import into Yealico

  1. Copy the JSON from iYegen (or share/export it).
  2. Open Yealico and import the rule from the clipboard/file.
  3. Open the site inside Yealico — list, detail and video/gallery are all wired.

One minute, start to finish — most of it spent watching the wand work. Advanced users can still open the hood: every automatic choice can be reviewed and adjusted by hand in the Advanced sections. But for most sites, most of the time, you will never need to.
